# 0.3.0 plan

What 0.3.0 is for: making the accuracy claims checkable. 0.2.0's figures come from one machine,
are stated without error bars, and miss cases a comparison change could break. 0.3.0 first closes
those gaps in the benchmark, then fixes issue #2 against the stronger benchmark, then tries one
change to the comparison, global sub-pixel registration, and keeps it only if the numbers say so.

Every item below says what will be measured, the result that would reject it, and the files it
touches. Each rejection rule is written here before the data it applies to is collected, and is
not changed after. A change to the evaluation set, the labels or the scoring is a change to the
results, so it is reviewed as one: in its own pull request, with the baselines regenerated.

## Order

1. Benchmark gaps (i), in the order listed. They change no verdict of the package, only what is
   measured, so they go first: everything after is judged on them.
2. Issue #2 (ii), judged on the benchmark as it stands after (i).
3. Global sub-pixel registration (iii), judged on a held-out set frozen before any of its code
   runs.

The new pairs of (i-c) are merged before any candidate for (ii) or (iii) is judged, so that both
are judged on the cases they are most likely to break.

## (i) Benchmark gaps

### (i-a) The accuracy benchmark in CI on Linux, macOS and Windows, with each table published

Today the judge runs in CI on Linux, but the 172 rendered pairs are compared only on a machine like
the one the baseline was made on (macOS, Apple M4), by the maintainer, before a release. The tables
in [the accuracy page](../benchmarks/accuracy.md) are macOS only.

- **Change.** The judge job runs on pinned runner images for each platform (Ubuntu, macOS,
  Windows). Each run publishes its accuracy table to the job summary and its result file as an
  artifact. Each platform gets its own committed baseline, made by CI on that image, and
  `check-baseline.mjs` compares rendered pairs against the baseline of the platform it runs on.
  The accuracy page gets one section per platform, generated from the CI result files by a script.
- **Measurement.** Two runs of the same commit on the same runner image, on different days: the
  SHA-256 of every rendered PNG, and every verdict.
- **Rejects it, per platform.** Any rendered pair whose bytes differ between the two runs. That
  platform's rendered pairs are then reported in its table but not enforced, and the platform is
  listed as not reproducible, with the pairs that moved. (Pairs that move between runs on one image
  are self-noise, item (i-d), not a benchmark failure to hide.)
- **Files.** `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, `bench/check-baseline.mjs`, `bench/judge.mjs` (table
  output), a new `bench/tables.mjs` (markdown from result files),
  `bench/results/judge-baseline-0.3.0-<platform>.json`, `docs/benchmarks/accuracy.md`,
  `CONTRIBUTING.md` (the judge section).
- **Note.** The GPU-against-software pairs need a GPU. Whether the macOS runner exposes one to
  Chromium is measured, not assumed; without one those 16 pairs stay on the maintainer's machine.

### (i-b) Wilson 95% intervals on every rate

With 9 to 24 pairs per group, one pair moves a rate by 4 to 11 points. For example, 9 of 15
sub-pixel shifts failing is 60%, with a 95% interval of 36% to 80%.

- **Change.** Every rate the benchmark and the judge print carries its Wilson score interval
  (Wilson, 1927) at 95%: the accuracy tables, the judge's accuracy summary, and the accuracy page.
- **Measurement.** The interval function against published values: Newcombe (1998), method 3,
  gives 81 of 263 as 0.2553 to 0.3662 and 15 of 148 as 0.0624 to 0.1605, and 0 of n has an upper
  bound of z²/(n + z²).
- **Rejects it.** Any of those values off by more than 0.0001.
- **How the intervals are read.** A difference between two builds on the same pairs is not judged
  by whether their intervals overlap: the pairs are the same, so the comparison is paired. Builds
  are compared by the pairs one gets right and the other wrong (as the judge already lists), with
  an exact sign test where a claim of improvement is made, as in (iii).
- **Files.** A new `bench/stats.mjs` and its declaration file, `test/bench/stats.test.ts`,
  `bench/accuracy.mjs`, `bench/judge.mjs`, `docs/benchmarks/accuracy.md`, the README's test count.

### (i-c) Should-fail pairs the benchmark is missing

No pair removes a one-pixel line, changes how text is anti-aliased or hinted, or is drawn in a
dark theme. Spatial filtering, or any change that sets more pixels aside as noise, could hide
exactly these, so they must exist before such a change is tried.

- **Change.** New should-fail pairs, labelled by construction:
  - **A removed one-pixel line**: an overlay outline over textured ground (crisp, and anti-aliased
    across two pixels), a grid line on a flat chart panel, and a divider in a dark theme. Also
    rendered in all three browsers, at 1× and 2×: a grid line removed from the chart page and an
    overlay outline removed from the 2D map page.
  - **Text anti-aliasing**: the same text with subpixel (RGB stripe) anti-aliasing and with
    greyscale anti-aliasing.
  - **Font hinting**: the same text with stems snapped to whole pixels and drawn at their true
    positions and widths.
  - **Dark themes**: a dark panel whose text is dimmed, and whose background is a shade lighter.

  Text anti-aliasing and hinting cannot be switched the same way in every browser on every
  platform, so those pairs are generated from scratch (deterministic, the same bytes everywhere),
  like the existing synthetic set, and are checked in CI on every platform.

- **Measurement.** For each pair: how many pixels change, and the largest ΔE00 among them, with
  every noise class counted; then every comparator's verdict, at no allowance and at 0.1%.
- **Rejects a pair (it is dropped, not relabelled).** Fewer than 20 pixels change, or no changed
  pixel reaches ΔE00 3, the benchmark's floor for a visible regression. A rendered variant that
  draws the same bytes as its page in a browser is left out for that browser.
- **Not a rejection.** 0.2.0 missing some of them. The point is to measure; a miss is recorded in
  the baseline as a wrong verdict and becomes a target.
- **Files.** A new `bench/cases.mjs` and its declaration file, `bench/corpus.mjs`,
  `bench/accuracy.mjs`, `bench/judge.mjs`, `bench/judge-labels.json`,
  `bench/results/judge-baseline-0.2.0.json` (regenerated with the new labels),
  `test/browser/fixtures/chart.html`, `test/browser/fixtures/map.html`,
  `test/compare/cases.test.ts`, `docs/benchmarks/accuracy.md`.

### (i-d) Self-noise: each page rendered N times in the same browser

The benchmark renders each page once. WebKit's map has been seen to differ between two loads by
two pixels at ΔE00 1.05, so some pairs labelled "should match" may fail for reasons no change
caused, and some regressions may pass for the same reason.

- **Change.** Each benchmark page, in each browser, at 1× and 2×, rendered N = 10 times in one
  browser, each in a fresh page. All 45 pairs of each page are compared by every comparator, and
  reported as a self-noise set: per page and browser, the pairs that are not byte-identical and the
  pairs each comparator fails.
- **Hypothesis.** Every page renders byte-identically ten times in one browser.
- **Rejects the hypothesis for a page.** Any of its 45 pairs not byte-identical. That page's pairs
  enter the benchmark as should-match self-noise, and its other pairs are reported with that
  floor beside them. Pairs within a page are not independent, so rates are given over pages, not
  pairs.
- **Files.** `bench/corpus.mjs` (repeat renders), `bench/accuracy.mjs`, `bench/judge.mjs`,
  `bench/judge-labels.json` (own pull request), `docs/benchmarks/accuracy.md`.

## (ii) Issue #2: a faint uniform change beside edges is set aside as shifts

A translucent overlay over textured ground made 5 points more transparent moves nothing. The issue
was filed against a draft of 0.2.0, which set aside a third of it as sub-pixel shifts. 0.2.0 as
released sets aside less, but still some: on the synthetic pair (`surfaceOpacity()` in
`bench/synthetic.mjs`), of 22,810 pixels past ΔE00 1 it counts 21,348 (93.6%) and sets aside 1,095
as shifts (4.8%), 239 as rounding and 128 as anti-aliasing. On the original capture, which is not in
this repository, it counts 82.7% (measured elsewhere). The issue places the pixels set aside along
edges, where a move can carry the most colour across a window's border.

- **Change.** Per [CONTRIBUTING](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#new-detector-designs): the issue is the
  design discussion; each approach (fitting the move by least squares on the gradient, larger
  windows, or registration first) is built on its own branch and judged by the same judge,
  unchanged, then compared with `judge.mjs --compare`. The reasoning goes in the design log.
- **Measurement.** The judge's accuracy section on every set, its issue #2 residual, and the
  margin on pairs that should match.
- **Rejects a candidate.** Any one of:
  - a verdict lost against 0.2.0, at either tolerance, on any set, including the pairs of (i-c);
  - more than 1% of the synthetic opacity pair's pixels past ΔE00 1 (228 of 22,810) still set
    aside as shifts (0.2.0: 1,095);
  - the "textured ground beside an edge, three levels darker" pair still passing with no
    allowance;
  - the worst margin over should-match pairs allowed 10 pixels or more rising by more than 10
    points over 0.2.0's on the same run;
  - whole-frame comparison more than 5% slower than 0.1.0 (the judge's condition 3).
- **Reported, not gating.** The share counted on the original capture, which is not in this
  repository; it is given as measured elsewhere.
- **Files.** `src/image/shift.ts`, possibly `src/compare/comparator.ts`,
  `test/image/shift.test.ts`, `test/compare/findings.test.ts` (its bound tightened, not loosened),
  `docs/design/shift-tolerance.md`, `docs/roadmap.md` (known gap 6), `CHANGELOG.md`.

If registration (iii) turns out to be the approach that fixes the issue, the two land as one
change that must pass both items' rules.

## (iii) Global sub-pixel registration

A map panned by part of a pixel moves the whole image by the same amount. Phase correlation
(Kuglin and Hines, 1975), refined to a fraction of a pixel (Guizar-Sicairos, Thurman and Fienup,
2008), estimates that translation; the comparison then shifts one image by it before comparing, and
reports the shift found, so a test can also fail on movement above a limit.

- **A fresh held-out set first.** The current held-out set (2×) was measured while 0.2.0's rules
  were developed, so it is no longer unseen for them. Before any registration code runs against
  rendered pairs, a new held-out set is rendered and its labels committed: the same pages at 1.5×
  device scale (different rasterisation; the shifts become 0.38 to 0.45 device pixels), with each
  category of the 1× set. Nobody looks at its per-pair results until a candidate is frozen.
- **Component checks, before any verdict is looked at.**
  - On the synthetic surface panned by known amounts (0.1 to 0.9 px in x and y), the estimate is
    within 0.05 px of the true shift, root mean square.
  - On every should-fail pair in which nothing moved (colour changes, the pairs of (i-c)), the
    estimate is under 0.02 px, so registration has nothing to explain away.

  Missing either stops the item.

- **Measurement.** The judge on every set, at both tolerances, against 0.2.0 (or 0.2.0 plus (ii),
  if that has landed), paired pair by pair; and `npm run bench`.
- **Adopt it only if all of:**
  - no verdict is lost on any set at either tolerance; in particular, no should-fail pair newly
    passes;
  - on the fresh held-out set, at least 5 verdicts are gained (with none lost, an exact one-sided
    sign test then gives p ≤ 0.031); fewer gained is not an improvement this benchmark can show;
  - comparing a fully changed 1920×1080 image is no more than 25% slower than without it.
- **Rejects it.** Failing any of those. It then stays out of the release, the attempt is written
  up in a design log, and the figures go in the roadmap.
- **Files.** A new `src/image/registration.ts`, `src/compare/comparator.ts`, `src/defaults.ts`,
  `src/compare/types.ts` (the shift found, in the result), `src/index.ts`,
  `test/image/registration.test.ts`, `bench/corpus.mjs` (the 1.5× set),
  `bench/judge-labels.json` (own pull request), a new `docs/design/registration.md`,
  `docs/guides/usage.md`, `docs/roadmap.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Not in 0.3.0

- **Fingerprints for recurring differences.** Recognising a difference seen before needs the
  self-noise data of (i-d) to say what recurs and how often, and its main risk, a real change
  landing on a known fingerprint, can only be measured once the should-fail pairs of (i-c) exist.
  Both arrive in this release; the measurement comes after.
- **Spatial filtering before the colour difference.** It changes every verdict at once, and the
  case it is most likely to break, a removed one-pixel line, enters the benchmark only in this
  release. It needs its own design issue, judged on these pairs, after 0.3.0.
- **Local registration (optical flow), a visibility model, other colour differences, regions and
  layers, and the speed work** in the roadmap: each changes the comparison, and this release
  allows one such change.
- **A labelling study with observers.** Labels stay by construction; the study is still the way to
  test them.
